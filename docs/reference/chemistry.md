## General Chemistry 1

### Topic 1: Atomic Structure and Periodicity

**Q1.** `[C]` Explain the difference between the Bohr model and the quantum mechanical model of the atom.
> **Answer:** Bohr model: electrons in fixed circular orbits (shells). Quantum model: electrons in orbitals (probability distributions described by wavefunctions). The Bohr model works well for hydrogen but fails for multi-electron atoms.

**Q2.** `[C]` Why does atomic radius generally decrease from left to right across a period?
> **Answer:** Across a period, nuclear charge increases while electrons are added to the same shell. The increased effective nuclear charge ($Z_{eff}$) pulls the valence electrons closer, shrinking the radius.

**Q3.** `[C]` Explain why the first ionization energy of nitrogen is higher than that of oxygen.
> **Answer:** Nitrogen has a half-filled $2p$ subshell ($2p^3$) which is particularly stable. Oxygen has $2p^4$, and removing one electron from its doubly occupied $2p$ orbital relieves electron-electron repulsion, making ionization easier.

**Q4.** `[P]` Calculate the wavelength of light emitted when an electron in hydrogen transitions from $n=4$ to $n=2$.
> **Answer:** $1/\lambda=R_H(1/n_1^2-1/n_2^2)=1.097\times10^7(1/4-1/16)=1.097\times10^7\times3/16=2.056\times10^6\text{m}^{-1}$. $\lambda=486\text{nm}$ (blue Balmer line).

**Q5.** `[P]` How many electrons can the $d$ subshell hold? How many orbitals are in a $4f$ subshell?
> **Answer:** $d$ subshell has 5 orbitals × 2 electrons = 10 electrons. $4f$ subshell has 7 orbitals.

**Q6.** `[P]` Write the full electron configuration for $\text{Fe}$ (atomic number 26) and for $\text{Fe}^{2+}$.
> **Answer:** Fe: $[\text{Ar}]3d^64s^2$. Fe²⁺: $[\text{Ar}]3d^6$ (the $4s$ electrons are removed first).

**Q7.** `[P]` Arrange the following in order of increasing atomic radius: $\text{Na}$, $\text{Al}$, $\text{Cl}$, $\text{K}$.
> **Answer:** $\text{Cl} < \text{Al} < \text{Na} < \text{K}$ (increasing $Z$ across period decreases radius; increasing period increases radius).

**Q8.** `[CT]` Predict which has the higher melting point: $\text{NaCl}$ or $\text{MgO}$. Explain.
> **Answer:** MgO. It has 2+ and 2− ions vs NaCl's 1+ and 1−. The stronger electrostatic attraction (Lattice energy $\propto q_1q_2/r$) in MgO requires more energy to disrupt, resulting in a higher melting point (~2852°C vs ~801°C).

**Q9.** `[CT]` Use VSEPR to predict the geometry and bond angle of $\text{NH}_3$, $\text{H}_2\text{O}$, and $\text{BF}_3$.
> **Answer:** $\text{NH}_3$: 3 bonding pairs + 1 lone pair → trigonal pyramidal, ~107°. $\text{H}_2\text{O}$: 2 bonding + 2 lone → bent, ~104.5°. $\text{BF}_3$: 3 bonding + 0 lone → trigonal planar, 120°.

**Q10.** `[N]` An element has the electron configuration $[\text{Ar}]3d^{10}4s^24p^3$. Identify the element, give its period and group, and predict its most common oxidation state.
> **Answer:** $[\text{Ar}]3d^{10}4s^24p^3$ is Arsenic (As). Period 4, Group 15 (VA). Most common oxidation states: +3 and +5 (also −3).

**Q11.** `[N]` Explain why chromium (Cr) has the electron configuration $[\text{Ar}]3d^54s^1$ instead of $[\text{Ar}]3d^44s^2$.
> **Answer:** A half-filled $3d$ subshell ($3d^5$) is particularly stable due to exchange energy (all spins parallel, maximizing favorable electron exchange interactions). The small energy gain from this stability outweighs the cost of promoting one $4s$ electron to $3d$.

**Q12.** `[C]` Rank $\text{F}$, $\text{Cl}$, $\text{Br}$, and $\text{I}$ in order of decreasing electron affinity and explain the trend.
> **Answer:** $\text{Cl} > \text{F} > \text{Br} > \text{I}$. Fluorine is anomalously low due to its small size causing electron-electron repulsion in the compact $2p$ shell. Generally, electron affinity decreases down a group.

### Topic 2: Stoichiometry and Limiting Reactants

**Q13.** `[C]` Define molarity and explain how it differs from molality.
> **Answer:** Molarity ($M$) = moles/liter of *solution*. Molality ($m$) = moles/kilogram of *solvent*. Molality is temperature-independent (mass doesn't change with $T$), while molarity is not (volume changes with $T$).

**Q14.** `[P]` Balance the following equation: $\text{Al} + \text{O}_2 \rightarrow \text{Al}_2\text{O}_3$.
> **Answer:** $4\text{Al} + 3\text{O}_2 \rightarrow 2\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3$.

**Q15.** `[P]` What mass of $\text{CO}_2$ is produced when $25.0\text{g}$ of $\text{CH}_4$ is burned completely? ($\text{CH}_4+2\text{O}_2\rightarrow\text{CO}_2+2\text{H}_2\text{O}$)
> **Answer:** Moles CH₄ = 25.0/16.0 = 1.5625 mol. 1:1 mole ratio → 1.5625 mol CO₂. Mass = 1.5625 × 44.0 = 68.75 g.

**Q16.** `[P]` A $3.00\text{g}$ sample of $\text{H}_2$ is mixed with $29.0\text{g}$ of $\text{N}_2$. Find the limiting reagent and grams of $\text{NH}_3$ produced ($\text{N}_2+3\text{H}_2\rightarrow 2\text{NH}_3$).
> **Answer:** mol H₂ = 3.0/2.0 = 1.5. mol N₂ = 29.0/28.0 ≈ 1.036. Need 3 mol H₂ per mol N₂. H₂ available: 1.5 mol → needs 0.5 mol N₂. N₂ available: 1.036 mol. H₂ is limiting. 1.5 mol H₂ → 1.0 mol NH₃ = 17.0 g.

**Q17.** `[P]` What volume of $0.500\text{M}$ $\text{NaOH}$ is needed to neutralize $25.0\text{mL}$ of $0.750\text{M}$ $\text{HCl}$?
> **Answer:** mol HCl = 0.0250 × 0.750 = 0.01875 mol. Needs equal mol NaOH. V = 0.01875/0.500 = 0.0375 L = 37.5 mL.

**Q18.** `[P]` A $1.50\text{g}$ sample of a compound burns to give $2.60\text{g}$ $\text{CO}_2$ and $1.60\text{g}$ $\text{H}_2\text{O}$. Find the empirical formula. (The compound contains only C, H, and possibly O.)
> **Answer:** mol CO₂ = 2.60/44.0 = 0.0591 mol C (= 0.710 g C). mol H₂O = 1.60/18.0 = 0.0889 mol → 2×0.0889 = 0.178 mol H (= 0.178 g H). O = 1.50−0.710−0.178 = 0.612 g O = 0.0383 mol. C:H:O = 0.0591:0.178:0.0383 = 1.54:4.65:1 ≈ 3:9:2. Empirical formula: $\text{C}_3\text{H}_9\text{O}_2$... re-check: 0.0591/0.0383 = 1.54, 0.178/0.0383 = 4.65. Ratio ≈ 2:6:1. Empirical: $\text{C}_2\text{H}_6\text{O}$.

**Q19.** `[CT]` In a double replacement reaction, $\text{Pb(NO}_3)_2\text{(aq)}$ is added to $\text{KI(aq)}$. What precipitate forms? Write the net ionic equation.
> **Answer:** Possible precipitate: PbI₂ (lead(II) iodide — insoluble). $\text{Pb}^{2+}(aq)+2\text{I}^-(aq)\rightarrow\text{PbI}_2(s)$.

**Q20.** `[CT]` A student performs a synthesis and obtains $18.3\text{g}$ of product. The theoretical yield is $22.5\text{g}$. Calculate the percent yield. Then, if she uses the product in a second step with 90% yield, what is the overall yield for both steps?
> **Answer:** Step 1 yield = 18.3/22.5 × 100% = 81.3%. Overall = 81.3% × 90% = 73.2%.

**Q21.** `[N]` A solution has $[\text{H}^+]=3.5\times10^{-4}\text{M}$. Calculate its pH and pOH and determine whether it is acidic or basic.
> **Answer:** $\text{pH}=-\log(3.5\times10^{-4})=3.46$. $\text{pOH}=14-3.46=10.54$. Acidic (pH < 7).

**Q22.** `[N]` Why does a 1 M solution of acetic acid have a higher pH than a 1 M solution of HCl?
> **Answer:** HCl is a strong acid and dissociates completely: $[\text{H}^+]=1\text{M}$. Acetic acid is weak and only partially dissociates ($K_a=1.8\times10^{-5}$), so $[\text{H}^+]\ll1\text{M}$ and pH is much higher.

### Topic 3: Gas Laws

**Q23.** `[C]` State the postulates of the Kinetic Molecular Theory and explain how each gives rise to an observable macroscopic property.

**Q24.** `[C]` How does the Ideal Gas Law fail for real gases at high pressure and low temperature?
> **Answer:** At high pressure, molecules are close together and intermolecular attractions become significant (lowers $P$), and molecular volume is no longer negligible (increases $V$). At low temperatures, kinetic energy is too low to overcome attractions, leading to condensation.

**Q25.** `[P]` A gas occupies $3.0\text{L}$ at $27°\text{C}$ and $2.0\text{atm}$. If it is heated to $127°\text{C}$ and the pressure drops to $1.5\text{atm}$, what is the new volume?
> **Answer:** $P_1V_1/T_1=P_2V_2/T_2$. $V_2=P_1V_1T_2/(T_1P_2)=2.0(3.0)(400)/(300\times1.5)=\frac{2400}{450}=5.33\text{L}$.

**Q26.** `[P]` A gas sample contains $0.40\text{mol}$ $\text{N}_2$, $0.20\text{mol}$ $\text{O}_2$, and $0.10\text{mol}$ $\text{CO}_2$ at total pressure $1.40\text{atm}$. Find the partial pressure of each gas.
> **Answer:** $\chi_{N_2}=0.40/0.70=4/7$. $P_{N_2}=0.800\text{atm}$. $\chi_{O_2}=2/7$. $P_{O_2}=0.400\text{atm}$. $\chi_{CO_2}=1/7$. $P_{CO_2}=0.200\text{atm}$.

**Q27.** `[P]` What is the root mean square speed of $\text{O}_2$ molecules at $25°\text{C}$?
> **Answer:** $v_{rms}=\sqrt{3RT/M}=\sqrt{3(8.314)(298)/0.032}\approx\sqrt{231,758}\approx481\text{m/s}$.

**Q28.** `[P]` Under what conditions would $0.500\text{mol}$ of a real gas most behave ideally?
> **Answer:** High temperature and low pressure — conditions where the volume is large relative to molecular size and kinetic energy dominates over intermolecular forces.

**Q29.** `[CT]` Calculate the molar volume of $\text{CO}_2$ using the van der Waals equation at $300\text{K}$ and $10\text{atm}$ ($a=3.592\text{L}^2\text{atm/mol}^2$, $b=0.0427\text{L/mol}$). Compare to ideal gas.
> **Answer:** Van der Waals: $(P+a/V_m^2)(V_m-b)=RT$. Ideal: $V_m=RT/P=2.46\text{L/mol}$. Use iterative substitution. Start with ideal as initial guess, then correct: $V_m\approx2.37\text{L/mol}$ (slightly less than ideal — attractions dominate at these conditions).

**Q30.** `[N]` An unknown gas effuses through a small hole in $15\text{s}$. Under identical conditions, $\text{O}_2$ effuses in $10\text{s}$. What is the molar mass of the unknown gas?
> **Answer:** Graham's Law: $r_1/r_2=\sqrt{M_2/M_1}$. $(10/15)^2=M_{O_2}/M_{unk}=32/M_{unk}$. $M_{unk}=32\times(15/10)^2=32\times2.25=72\text{g/mol}$.

### Topic 4: Thermochemistry and Calorimetry

**Q31.** `[C]` Explain the difference between $q$ and $\Delta H$. Under what conditions does $q_{rxn}=\Delta H_{rxn}$?
> **Answer:** $q$ is heat exchanged by any process. $\Delta H=q$ only at constant pressure. In a bomb calorimeter (constant volume), $q_V=\Delta U\neq\Delta H$ in general.

**Q32.** `[P]` Calculate $\Delta H_{rxn}$ for $\text{C(s)} + \frac{1}{2}\text{O}_2\text{(g)} \rightarrow \text{CO(g)}$ using Hess's Law:
- $\text{C(s)} + \text{O}_2\text{(g)} \rightarrow \text{CO}_2\text{(g)}$, $\Delta H=-393.5\text{kJ}$
- $\text{CO(g)} + \frac{1}{2}\text{O}_2\text{(g)} \rightarrow \text{CO}_2\text{(g)}$, $\Delta H=-283.0\text{kJ}$
> **Answer:** Subtract second from first: $\Delta H=-393.5-(-283.0)=-110.5\text{kJ/mol}$.

**Q33.** `[P]` A $50.0\text{g}$ piece of copper ($c=0.385\text{J/g°C}$) at $120°\text{C}$ is placed in $100\text{g}$ of water at $20°\text{C}$. Find the equilibrium temperature.
> **Answer:** $m_{Cu}c_{Cu}(T_{eq}-120)=-m_w c_w(T_{eq}-20)$. $50(0.385)(T_{eq}-120)=-100(4.18)(T_{eq}-20)$. $19.25T_{eq}-2310=-418T_{eq}+8360$. $437.25T_{eq}=10670$. $T_{eq}\approx24.4°\text{C}$.

**Q34.** `[P]` Use standard enthalpies of formation to calculate $\Delta H°$ for combustion of glucose: $\text{C}_6\text{H}_{12}\text{O}_6+6\text{O}_2\rightarrow6\text{CO}_2+6\text{H}_2\text{O}$.
($\Delta H_f°$: glucose = −1274.5 kJ/mol, CO₂ = −393.5 kJ/mol, H₂O(l) = −285.8 kJ/mol)
> **Answer:** $\Delta H° = [6(-393.5)+6(-285.8)] - [-1274.5+0] = -2361-1714.8+1274.5 = -2801.3\text{kJ/mol}$.

**Q35.** `[CT]` A bomb calorimeter has heat capacity $C_{cal}=7.50\text{kJ/°C}$. Combustion of $0.500\text{g}$ benzoic acid raises temp by $1.94°\text{C}$. If the known $\Delta U_{comb}=-26.4\text{kJ/g}$ for benzoic acid, what is the heat capacity from this experiment?
> **Answer:** $q=0.500\times26.4=13.2\text{kJ}$. $C_{cal}=q/\Delta T=13.2/1.94\approx6.80\text{kJ/°C}$.

**Q36.** `[CT]` Calculate $\Delta G°$ for a reaction with $\Delta H°=-200\text{kJ/mol}$ and $\Delta S°=-150\text{J/mol K}$ at $400\text{K}$. Is the reaction spontaneous?
> **Answer:** $\Delta G°=\Delta H°-T\Delta S°=-200-(400)(-0.150)=-200+60=-140\text{kJ/mol}$. $\Delta G°<0$ so spontaneous at 400 K.

**Q37.** `[N]` At what temperature does the reaction in Q36 become non-spontaneous?
> **Answer:** At the crossover, $\Delta G°=0$: $T=\Delta H°/\Delta S°=-200\text{kJ}/(-0.150\text{kJ/K})=1333\text{K}$. Above 1333 K it becomes non-spontaneous.

**Q38.** `[N]` The dissolution of ammonium nitrate ($\Delta H>0$) is used in cold packs. Why does the pack get cold if the process is endothermic?
> **Answer:** The endothermic dissolution absorbs heat from the surroundings (your hand). The process is driven by a large positive $\Delta S$ (disorder increases on dissolution), making $\Delta G<0$ at room temperature.

## Qualitative Analysis

### Topic 5: Solubility Rules and Precipitation

**Q39.** `[C]` State the solubility rules for sulfates and hydroxides. Give one example of each class of insoluble compound.
> **Answer:** Sulfates: soluble except $\text{BaSO}_4$, $\text{PbSO}_4$, $\text{SrSO}_4$, $\text{CaSO}_4$ (slightly). Hydroxides: insoluble except alkali metals and $\text{Ba(OH)}_2$, $\text{Sr(OH)}_2$, $\text{Ca(OH)}_2$ (slightly).

**Q40.** `[P]` Predict the products and write the complete and net ionic equations when $\text{Na}_2\text{CO}_3\text{(aq)}$ is mixed with $\text{CaCl}_2\text{(aq)}$.
> **Answer:** Complete: $2\text{Na}^++\text{CO}_3^{2-}+\text{Ca}^{2+}+2\text{Cl}^-\rightarrow\text{CaCO}_3(s)+2\text{Na}^++2\text{Cl}^-$. Net ionic: $\text{Ca}^{2+}+\text{CO}_3^{2-}\rightarrow\text{CaCO}_3(s)$.

**Q41.** `[P]` A solution may contain $\text{Ag}^+$, $\text{Ba}^{2+}$, and $\text{Fe}^{3+}$. When dilute $\text{HCl}$ is added, a white precipitate forms. Which cation is present?
> **Answer:** $\text{Ag}^+$ forms $\text{AgCl}(s)$ (white, insoluble in dilute HCl). $\text{BaCl}_2$ and $\text{FeCl}_3$ are soluble.

**Q42.** `[P]` A white precipitate forms when $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$ is added to a solution. The precipitate is insoluble in $\text{HNO}_3$. Which cation might be present?
> **Answer:** $\text{Ba}^{2+}$ or $\text{Pb}^{2+}$ (both form insoluble sulfates). $\text{AgSO}_4$ is slightly soluble and can be distinguished by adding $\text{HCl}$: $\text{Ag}^+$ gives a white chloride precipitate.

**Q43.** `[CT]` Outline a four-step separation scheme to separate and identify $\text{Ag}^+$, $\text{Pb}^{2+}$, $\text{Fe}^{3+}$, and $\text{K}^+$ from a single mixture.
> **Answer:**
> 1. Add dilute $\text{HCl}$: $\text{AgCl}(s)$ and $\text{PbCl}_2(s)$ precipitate. Centrifuge.
> 2. Wash precipitate with hot water: $\text{PbCl}_2$ dissolves (soluble hot), $\text{AgCl}$ remains. Confirm Pb²⁺ with $\text{K}_2\text{CrO}_4$ (yellow $\text{PbCrO}_4$) and Ag⁺ with $\text{NH}_3$ (dissolves to diaminosilver complex).
> 3. Decantate from step 1: Add $\text{NaOH}$. $\text{Fe(OH)}_3(s)$ (rust-brown) precipitates. Confirm with $\text{KSCN}$ (blood-red $\text{Fe(SCN)}^{2+}$).
> 4. Remaining solution: Confirm $\text{K}^+$ with flame test (lilac).

**Q44.** `[CT]` Explain why $\text{AgCl}$ dissolves in $\text{NH}_3$ solution but $\text{AgBr}$ does not readily do so.
> **Answer:** $\text{AgCl}$ has $K_{sp}=1.8\times10^{-10}$; $\text{AgBr}$ has $K_{sp}=5.0\times10^{-13}$ (much more insoluble). The formation of $[\text{Ag(NH}_3)_2]^+$ (formation constant $K_f=1.7\times10^7$) provides sufficient driving force to dissolve $\text{AgCl}$ but not the much less soluble $\text{AgBr}$.

**Q45.** `[N]` Calculate whether a precipitate will form when $50\text{mL}$ of $0.001\text{M}$ $\text{CaCl}_2$ is mixed with $50\text{mL}$ of $0.001\text{M}$ $\text{Na}_2\text{SO}_4$. ($K_{sp}(\text{CaSO}_4)=4.93\times10^{-5}$)
> **Answer:** After mixing, $[\text{Ca}^{2+}]=[\text{SO}_4^{2-}]=0.0005\text{M}$. $Q=[\text{Ca}^{2+}][\text{SO}_4^{2-}]=(0.0005)^2=2.5\times10^{-7}$. $Q<K_{sp}$. No precipitate.

**Q46.** `[N]` A solution of $0.10\text{M}$ $\text{AgNO}_3$ is slowly added to a solution containing $0.10\text{M}$ $\text{I}^-$ and $0.10\text{M}$ $\text{Cl}^-$. Which ion precipitates first? What is the concentration of the first ion when the second starts to precipitate? ($K_{sp}(\text{AgI})=8.5\times10^{-17}$, $K_{sp}(\text{AgCl})=1.8\times10^{-10}$)
> **Answer:** $\text{I}^-$ precipitates first (lower $K_{sp}$). $\text{AgCl}$ starts when $[\text{Ag}^+]=K_{sp}(\text{AgCl})/[\text{Cl}^-]=1.8\times10^{-9}\text{M}$. At this point, $[\text{I}^-]=K_{sp}(\text{AgI})/[\text{Ag}^+]=8.5\times10^{-17}/1.8\times10^{-9}=4.7\times10^{-8}\text{M}$ — effectively zero. Excellent separation!

### Topic 6: Ion Identification (Flame Tests and Reagents)

**Q47.** `[C]` Explain the quantum mechanical basis for flame tests.
> **Answer:** Flame excites electrons to higher energy levels. When they return to lower levels, photons of specific wavelengths are emitted, corresponding to the exact energy differences of that element's atomic structure. Each element has a unique set of spectral lines.

**Q48.** `[P]` Match each flame color to its ion: yellow-orange, lilac/violet, brick red, crimson red, pale green.
> **Answer:** Yellow-orange → $\text{Na}^+$; Lilac → $\text{K}^+$; Brick red → $\text{Ca}^{2+}$; Crimson → $\text{Sr}^{2+}$; Pale green → $\text{Ba}^{2+}$.

**Q49.** `[P]` An unknown solution gives a brick-red flame test. When $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$ is added, a white precipitate insoluble in dilute HCl forms. When $\text{CO}_3^{2-}$ is added, a white precipitate forms. Identify the solution.
> **Answer:** Brick-red flame: $\text{Ca}^{2+}$. White precipitate with $\text{SO}_4^{2-}$: $\text{CaSO}_4$ (slightly insoluble). White with $\text{CO}_3^{2-}$: $\text{CaCO}_3$. Solution contains calcium ions.

**Q50.** `[CT]` An unknown white powder is tested: (1) It dissolves in water to give a clear solution. (2) Flame test: yellow-orange. (3) Adding $\text{AgNO}_3$ gives no precipitate. (4) Adding $\text{BaCl}_2$ gives a white precipitate insoluble in dilute $\text{HCl}$. (5) The pH of the solution is 11. Identify the compound.
> **Answer:** (2) Na⁺ present. (3) No halide ($\text{Cl}^-$, $\text{Br}^-$, $\text{I}^-$). (4) $\text{BaSO}_4$ precipitate → $\text{SO}_4^{2-}$ present. (5) pH 11 → basic → but $\text{Na}_2\text{SO}_4$ solution is neutral... Re-examine. The compound might be $\text{Na}_2\text{SO}_4$ and the high pH is from residual $\text{NaOH}$ impurity. Or it could be $\text{Na}_2\text{CO}_3$ — but then step (4) BaCl₂ would give BaCO₃ (also white, insoluble in excess water but dissolves in acid). Distinguishing: if the precipitate in step (4) dissolves in dilute HCl with effervescence (CO₂), then carbonate. If it does not dissolve, sulfate. Given "insoluble in dilute HCl," the anion is $\text{SO}_4^{2-}$ and the compound is $\text{Na}_2\text{SO}_4$.

**Q51.** `[N]` A solution is initially colorless. Adding $\text{NaOH}$ produces a rust-brown precipitate. Adding $\text{KSCN}$ to the original solution turns it blood-red. When $\text{H}_2\text{S}$ is bubbled through the acidified solution, a black precipitate forms. Which metal ion is likely present? What is the black precipitate?
> **Answer:** Rust-brown with NaOH: $\text{Fe(OH)}_3$. Blood-red with KSCN: $[\text{Fe(SCN)}]^{2+}$. Black with $\text{H}_2\text{S}$: $\text{FeS}$? Actually, black precipitates with $\text{H}_2\text{S}$ are characteristic of $\text{PbS}$ (black), $\text{CuS}$ (black), or $\text{Bi}_2\text{S}_3$ (black). $\text{FeS}$ is actually black too, but $\text{Fe}^{3+}$ would be reduced. The combination of rust-brown hydroxide, blood-red thiocyanate, and black sulfide confirms $\text{Fe}^{3+}$.

**Q52.** `[N]` Explain why sodium contamination in glassware can interfere with flame tests for potassium, and how to compensate.
> **Answer:** $\text{Na}^+$ produces an intense yellow-orange flame that overwhelms the lilac of $\text{K}^+$. To compensate, view through a cobalt-blue glass filter — it absorbs yellow light but transmits the lilac from potassium, allowing confirmation even in the presence of sodium.

**Q53.** `[CT]` Design a systematic qualitative analysis scheme to identify whether a solution contains $\text{Cu}^{2+}$, $\text{Zn}^{2+}$, or both.
> **Answer:**
> 1. Observe color: $\text{Cu}^{2+}$ solutions are blue; $\text{Zn}^{2+}$ is colorless.
> 2. Add excess $\text{NaOH}$: Both precipitate as hydroxides. $\text{Zn(OH)}_2$ dissolves in excess NaOH (amphoteric → $[\text{Zn(OH)}_4]^{2-}$). Cu(OH)₂ does not dissolve.
> 3. Add $\text{NH}_3$: $\text{Cu(OH)}_2$ dissolves in excess NH₃ to form deep blue $[\text{Cu(NH}_3)_4]^{2+}$.
> 4. Confirm Zn²⁺ from the alkaline filtrate by acidifying and adding $\text{H}_2\text{S}$ (white ZnS) or diphenylthiocarbazone.
