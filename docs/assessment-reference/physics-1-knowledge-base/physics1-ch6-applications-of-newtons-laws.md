# Chapter 6: Applications of Newton's Laws

This chapter builds upon Newton's laws to solve more complex systems, particularly those involving friction, tension, drag forces, and centripetal forces.

## 6.1 Normal Force and Friction
*   **Normal Force ($\vec{N}$)**: The contact force exerted by a surface on an object, perpendicular to the surface. For a block on a horizontal surface, $N = mg$. On an incline of angle $\theta$, $N = mg \cos\theta$.
*   **Static Friction ($f_s$)**: The force that opposes the initiation of sliding motion.
    *   $f_s \leq \mu_s N$, where $\mu_s$ is the coefficient of static friction.
    *   The maximum static friction is $f_{s,\text{max}} = \mu_s N$.
*   **Kinetic Friction ($f_k$)**: The force that opposes the motion of two surfaces sliding past one another.
    *   $f_k = \mu_k N$, where $\mu_k$ is the coefficient of kinetic friction.

## 6.2 Tension and Springs
*   **Tension ($T$)**: The pulling force transmitted axially through a string, cable, or chain. Tension pulls outward along the rope from the object it is attached to.
    *   For a massless rope over a frictionless, massless pulley, tension is constant throughout the rope.
*   **Springs (Hooke's Law)**: $F_s = -k \Delta x$, where $k$ is the spring constant and $\Delta x$ is the displacement from equilibrium. The negative sign indicates it is a restoring force.

## 6.3 Drag Forces and Terminal Speed
*   **Drag Force ($F_D$)**: A frictional force exerted by fluids (like air or water) on moving objects.
    *   For low speeds: $F_D \propto v$ (Stokes' Law)
    *   For high speeds: $F_D = \frac{1}{2} C \rho A v^2$
*   **Terminal Speed ($v_t$)**: Reached when the drag force equals the driving force (e.g., gravity), resulting in zero net force and zero acceleration.
    *   $v_t = \sqrt{\frac{2mg}{C \rho A}}$

## 6.4 Centripetal Force
*   **Uniform Circular Motion**: An object moving in a circle of radius $r$ at constant speed $v$ experiences centripetal acceleration: $a_c = \frac{v^2}{r}$.
*   **Centripetal Force ($F_c$)**: Any net force causing uniform circular motion must point toward the center.
    *   $F_c = m a_c = m \frac{v^2}{r}$
    *   This is not a *new* force, but the required net force provided by tension, gravity, friction, etc.
