# Chapter 12: Static Equilibrium and Elasticity

This chapter examines the conditions under which rigid bodies remain at rest (or move with constant linear and angular velocity) and how they deform under stress.

## 12.1 Conditions for Static Equilibrium
For a rigid body to be in static equilibrium, it must satisfy two conditions:
1.  **Translational Equilibrium**: The net external force must be zero.
    $$ \sum \vec{F} = 0 $$
    In component form: $\sum F_x = 0$, $\sum F_y = 0$, $\sum F_z = 0$.
2.  **Rotational Equilibrium**: The net external torque about *any* pivot point must be zero.
    $$ \sum \vec{\tau} = 0 $$
    In component form: $\sum \tau_x = 0$, $\sum \tau_y = 0$, $\sum \tau_z = 0$.
    *Strategy Note*: You can choose *any* point as the axis of rotation for the torque equation. A smart choice of pivot point (often where unknown forces act) will eliminate those forces from the torque equation.

## 12.2 Center of Gravity
*   The **Center of Gravity (CG)** is the point at which the entire weight of the body can be considered to act for the purpose of calculating torque due to gravity.
*   In a uniform gravitational field, the center of gravity coincides with the **Center of Mass (CM)**.
*   $x_{cm} = \frac{\sum m_i x_i}{\sum m_i}$

## 12.3 Examples of Static Equilibrium
*   **The Balance of Torques**: Systems like a seesaw or a balanced mobile.
*   **Ladders and Hinges**: A ladder leaning against a wall involves friction at the floor, normal forces at the wall and floor, and gravity acting at the CM.
*   **Cables and Booms**: Structures supported by tension in cables and compression/tension in struts.

## 12.4 Elasticity, Stress, and Strain
*   **Stress**: The deforming force per unit area. $\text{Stress} = \frac{F}{A}$. (Units: Pascals, $\text{N/m}^2$)
*   **Strain**: The proportional deformation produced by stress. It is a dimensionless ratio. (e.g., $\frac{\Delta L}{L_0}$)
*   **Elastic Modulus**: In the linear elastic region (Hooke's Law), the ratio of stress to strain is constant.
    $$ \text{Elastic Modulus} = \frac{\text{Stress}}{\text{Strain}} $$
*   **Young's Modulus ($Y$ or $E$)**: For tensile or compressive stress. $Y = \frac{F/A}{\Delta L / L_0}$
*   **Shear Modulus ($S$ or $G$)**: For shear stress (forces parallel to surfaces). $S = \frac{F/A}{\Delta x / L_0}$
*   **Bulk Modulus ($B$)**: For volume stress (pressure changes). $B = -\frac{\Delta P}{\Delta V / V_0}$
