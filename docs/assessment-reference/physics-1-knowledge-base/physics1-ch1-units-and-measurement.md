# Chapter 1: Units and Measurement

## 1.1 The Scope and Scale of Physics
Physics is the foundational science that deals with the fundamental constituents of the universe, the forces they exert on one another, and the results of these forces. The scope of physics ranges from the subatomic scale (quarks and leptons) to the cosmic scale (galaxies and the universe itself). 

## 1.2 Units and Standards
To communicate measurements, physicists use the **International System of Units (SI)**.
The base units include:
- **Length**: meter (m)
- **Mass**: kilogram (kg)
- **Time**: second (s)
- **Electric Current**: ampere (A)
- **Thermodynamic Temperature**: kelvin (K)
- **Amount of Substance**: mole (mol)
- **Luminous Intensity**: candela (cd)

## 1.3 Unit Conversion
Measurements often need to be converted from one set of units to another. This is done using conversion factors, which are ratios of equivalent quantities expressed in different units. 
For example, to convert 60 mph to m/s:
$$ 60 \, \frac{\text{mi}}{\text{h}} \times \frac{1609 \, \text{m}}{1 \, \text{mi}} \times \frac{1 \, \text{h}}{3600 \, \text{s}} \approx 26.8 \, \text{m/s} $$

## 1.4 Dimensional Analysis
Dimensional analysis is a powerful tool used to check the mathematical relations of physical quantities. Every physical quantity can be expressed in terms of fundamental dimensions: Length ($L$), Mass ($M$), and Time ($T$).
For an equation to be physically meaningful, the dimensions on both sides must be identical.
For example, for $x = \frac{1}{2}at^2$:
$$ [x] = L $$
$$ [a] = \frac{L}{T^2}, \quad [t^2] = T^2 $$
$$ [at^2] = \frac{L}{T^2} \times T^2 = L $$

## 1.5 Estimates and Fermi Calculations
A Fermi estimate is a rough order-of-magnitude approximation of a quantity that is difficult or impossible to measure directly, based on reasonable assumptions.
*Example:* How many piano tuners are in Chicago? 
By estimating the population of Chicago, the fraction of households with pianos, the tuning frequency, and a tuner's work hours, one can derive a surprisingly accurate estimate.

## 1.6 Significant Figures
Significant figures indicate the precision of a measurement. 
- **Multiplication/Division**: The result must have the same number of significant figures as the measurement with the fewest significant figures.
- **Addition/Subtraction**: The result must have the same number of decimal places as the measurement with the fewest decimal places.
