# AoPS Geometry-Topic Expansion Matrix

This matrix is the authoring contract for three **Art of Problem Solving** subcategories: `aops-triangles`, `aops-circles-introduction`, and `aops-angle-chasing`. It does not apply to, alter, or rely on the separate Geometry category. All problem wording and diagrams must be original.

## Triangles (`aops-triangles`)

1. Triangle existence and right-triangle structure: inequality, Pythagorean theorem and converse.
2. Congruence and similarity: correspondence, valid criteria, scale factors, parallel-line similarity.
3. Centers, trigonometry, and area: incenter/circumcenter/centroid/orthocenter, right-triangle ratios, $\tfrac12bh$, $\tfrac12ab\sin C$, Heron, and $rs$.

Worked examples must include: feasibility/classification, Pythagorean/converse, congruence, similarity with a parallel line, altitude-to-hypotenuse, centers/loci, right-triangle trig, and all taught area forms.

## Circles (`aops-circles-introduction`)

1. Circle structure and measurement: center, radius, diameter, chord, arc, circumference, area.
2. Arcs, sectors, chords, and tangents: central angles, sector measures, scale laws, tangent-radius perpendicularity.

Worked examples must include: radius/diameter relationships, circumference/area, scaling, arc length, sector area, chord/central-angle reasoning, and tangent-radius proof/application.

## Angle Chasing (`aops-angle-chasing`)

1. Line, triangle, and parallel-line angle networks: vertical/linear/full-turn/triangle/exterior and converse rules.
2. Circle and mixed proof chases: central/inscribed/interior/exterior/tangent-chord angles, cyclic quadrilaterals, auxiliary radii and parallels.

Worked examples must include each theorem configuration and mixed chases combining parallel lines, isosceles triangles, cyclic quadrilaterals, tangents, and an explicitly justified auxiliary construction.

## Required assessment matrix per topic

- One concept lesson per module, each 6–7 sections with a multiple-choice check in each section and visual media in every lesson.
- One worked example per module, exactly three original problems, 4–6 guided steps per problem, and visual media for every problem.
- One glossary and one recall drill, covering new/unfamiliar terminology.
- Easy, hard, and Olympiad quizzes; easy, hard, and Olympiad tests. Every quiz/test has 15 questions, predominantly multiple choice. Tests can sample 15 questions from a deeper topic bank.
