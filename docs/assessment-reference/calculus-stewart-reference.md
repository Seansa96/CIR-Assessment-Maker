## Calculus 1 Reference Map

### Functions, Models, and Calculus Readiness

Core ideas:

- A function is a rule with input, output, domain, and range.
- Calculus problems often begin by translating a situation into a function or graph.
- Function transformations, composition, inverse functions, and units matter before differentiation begins.

Useful recall targets:

- Domain restrictions for radicals, rational expressions, logarithms, and inverse trig functions.
- Composition: $(f\circ g)(x)=f(g(x))$.
- Average rate of change: $\frac{f(b)-f(a)}{b-a}$.

Common misconceptions:

- Treating a graph as decoration instead of data.
- Ignoring units when interpreting slope or accumulation.
- Cancelling through addition, such as simplifying $\frac{x+2}{x}$ incorrectly.

Best assessment fit:

- Concept lessons for function modeling and graph interpretation.
- Recall drills for domain restrictions and transformation vocabulary.
- Worked examples for translating verbal data into functions.

### Limits and Continuity

Core ideas:

- A limit asks what value a function approaches, not necessarily what value it has.
- One-sided behavior controls two-sided limits.
- Continuity combines existence of $f(a)$, existence of $\lim_{x\to a} f(x)$, and equality between them.

Useful formulas and patterns:

- Difference quotient: $\frac{f(a+h)-f(a)}{h}$.
- Squeeze theorem structure: $g(x)\le f(x)\le h(x)$ and both outside functions approach the same value.
- Standard trig limit: $\lim_{x\to 0}\frac{\sin x}{x}=1$.

Common misconceptions:

- Substituting blindly into an indeterminate form.
- Assuming a graph has a limit because a point is filled in.
- Forgetting that infinite limits describe unbounded behavior, not a real-number limit.

Best assessment fit:

- Concept lessons for graphical and symbolic limits.
- Worked examples for factoring, rationalizing, trig limits, and squeeze theorem.
- Quizzes mixing graphs, tables, and algebraic expressions.

### Derivatives and Differentiation

Core ideas:

- The derivative is instantaneous rate of change and tangent slope.
- Differentiation rules compress repeated limit reasoning into reusable procedures.
- The chain rule is a structural rule: identify the outside function and inside function.

Useful formulas and patterns:

- Definition: $f'(a)=\lim_{h\to 0}\frac{f(a+h)-f(a)}{h}$.
- Product rule: $(fg)'=f'g+fg'$.
- Quotient rule: $\left(\frac{f}{g}\right)'=\frac{f'g-fg'}{g^2}$.
- Chain rule: $\frac{d}{dx}f(g(x))=f'(g(x))g'(x)$.
- Implicit differentiation: differentiate both sides with respect to $x$, treating $y$ as $y(x)$.

Common misconceptions:

- Applying the power rule to products or quotients without rewriting.
- Dropping the inner derivative in a chain rule problem.
- Forgetting $\frac{d}{dx}[y^n]=ny^{n-1}y'$ in implicit differentiation.

Best assessment fit:

- Recall drills for derivative rules.
- Worked examples for layered chain-rule recognition.
- Quizzes that force method selection before calculation.

### Applications of Derivatives

Core ideas:

- Derivatives encode monotonicity, local extrema, concavity, and optimization.
- Related rates use a relationship between variables before differentiating with respect to time.
- Linearization uses a tangent line as a local model.

Useful formulas and patterns:

- Linearization: $L(x)=f(a)+f'(a)(x-a)$.
- Newton's method: $x_{n+1}=x_n-\frac{f(x_n)}{f'(x_n)}$.
- Mean Value Theorem: if conditions hold, $f'(c)=\frac{f(b)-f(a)}{b-a}$ for some $c$.

Common misconceptions:

- Using endpoints incorrectly in absolute extrema problems.
- Optimizing before reducing the objective to one variable.
- Treating a critical point as automatically a maximum or minimum.

Best assessment fit:

- Worked examples for optimization and related rates.
- Concept lessons for graph analysis and theorem conditions.
- Tests combining setup, derivative calculation, and interpretation.

### Integrals and the Fundamental Theorem

Core ideas:

- Definite integrals measure signed accumulation.
- Antiderivatives reverse differentiation.
- The Fundamental Theorem of Calculus connects area accumulation and antiderivatives.

Useful formulas and patterns:

- Average value: $f_{\text{avg}}=\frac{1}{b-a}\int_a^b f(x)\,dx$.
- FTC Part 1: $\frac{d}{dx}\int_a^x f(t)\,dt=f(x)$.
- FTC Part 2: $\int_a^b f(x)\,dx=F(b)-F(a)$ when $F'=f$.
- Substitution pattern: choose $u=g(x)$ when $g'(x)$ is present or can be made present.

Common misconceptions:

- Forgetting signed area when a graph crosses the axis.
- Treating total area and net area as the same quantity.
- Choosing a substitution that does not simplify the whole integrand.

Best assessment fit:

- Concept lessons for accumulation.
- Worked examples for Riemann sums, FTC, and substitution.
- Recall drills for antiderivatives and substitution patterns.

## Calculus 2 Reference Map

### Integration Techniques

Core ideas:

- Technique selection is usually more important than mechanical integration.
- Integration by parts reverses the product rule.
- Trig integrals often depend on parity and identity conversion.
- Trig substitution transforms radicals using triangle identities.
- Partial fractions decomposes rational functions after division when needed.

Useful formulas and patterns:

- Integration by parts: $\int u\,dv=uv-\int v\,du$.
- Pythagorean identity: $\sin^2 x+\cos^2 x=1$.
- Secant identity: $\tan^2 x+1=\sec^2 x$.
- Partial fractions require proper rational functions first.

Common misconceptions:

- Trying partial fractions before polynomial division.
- Choosing $u$ in integration by parts so the new integral becomes harder.
- Using trig substitution without matching the radical pattern.

Best assessment fit:

- Concept lessons for the technique decision tree.
- Worked examples for each major family and mixed strategy selection.
- Recall drills for common antiderivatives, identities, and substitution forms.

### Applications of Integration

Core ideas:

- The hard part is usually identifying the infinitesimal slice.
- Area, volume, work, mass, hydrostatic force, and arc length all come from accumulation.
- Units help reveal whether the setup is plausible.

Useful formulas and patterns:

- Area between curves: $\int_a^b(\text{upper}-\text{lower})\,dx$.
- Washer volume: $\pi\int_a^b(R^2-r^2)\,dx$.
- Shell volume: $2\pi\int_a^b(\text{radius})(\text{height})\,dx$.
- Arc length: $\int_a^b\sqrt{1+(y')^2}\,dx$.
- Surface area: $2\pi\int_a^b(\text{radius})\sqrt{1+(y')^2}\,dx$.

Common misconceptions:

- Mixing shell and washer geometry.
- Using radius where diameter is needed.
- Forgetting that work is force times distance, with force often varying by position.

Best assessment fit:

- Visual concept lessons.
- Worked examples with diagrams and slice identification.
- Tests that require choosing the setup, not only evaluating it.

### Improper Integrals

Core ideas:

- Improper integrals are limits of proper integrals.
- Infinite intervals and infinite discontinuities are different triggers.
- Convergence may be determined by direct evaluation or by comparison.

Useful formulas and patterns:

- Type 1: replace an infinite bound with a finite variable and take a limit.
- Type 2: split at vertical asymptotes or discontinuities.
- $p$-test: $\int_1^\infty\frac{1}{x^p}\,dx$ converges if $p>1$.

Common misconceptions:

- Evaluating directly at infinity.
- Failing to split when a discontinuity lies inside the interval.
- Declaring convergence because an antiderivative exists.

Best assessment fit:

- Recall drills for type identification and $p$-test conditions.
- Worked examples for split intervals and comparison.
- Quizzes mixing convergence classification and exact evaluation.

### Sequences and Series

Core ideas:

- A sequence is a list of terms; a series is a sum of terms.
- Series convergence is decided by the behavior of partial sums.
- Test selection depends on form, positivity, alternating signs, factorials, powers, and comparison targets.

Useful formulas and patterns:

- Geometric series: $\sum ar^n$ converges when $|r|<1$.
- Integral test connects positive decreasing series to improper integrals.
- Alternating series test requires decreasing terms approaching zero.
- Ratio test is useful for factorials and exponential powers.
- Taylor series: $f(x)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{f^{(n)}(a)}{n!}(x-a)^n$ when valid.

Common misconceptions:

- Confusing $\lim a_n=0$ as sufficient for convergence.
- Using alternating series test without checking decreasing behavior.
- Forgetting radius and interval of convergence are separate tasks.

Best assessment fit:

- Concept lessons for convergence meaning and test selection.
- Recall drills for standard series and test conditions.
- Worked examples that force the learner to justify the chosen test.

### Parametric and Polar Curves

Core ideas:

- Parametric equations describe position through a parameter.
- Polar equations describe distance from origin as a function of angle.
- Calculus formulas must be built through $x(t),y(t)$ or $r(\theta)$ rather than copied from Cartesian form.

Useful formulas and patterns:

- Parametric slope: $\frac{dy}{dx}=\frac{dy/dt}{dx/dt}$.
- Parametric arc length: $\int_a^b\sqrt{(x')^2+(y')^2}\,dt$.
- Polar conversion: $x=r\cos\theta$, $y=r\sin\theta$.
- Polar area: $\frac12\int_\alpha^\beta r^2\,d\theta$.
- Polar slope: $\frac{dy}{dx}=\frac{r'\sin\theta+r\cos\theta}{r'\cos\theta-r\sin\theta}$.

Common misconceptions:

- Ignoring parameter bounds after eliminating the parameter.
- Treating $dr/d\theta$ as Cartesian slope.
- Choosing a random angle for polar tangent problems instead of solving for angles.

Best assessment fit:

- Graph recognition quizzes.
- Worked examples with generated media.
- Concept lessons for coordinate conversion and tangent conditions.
