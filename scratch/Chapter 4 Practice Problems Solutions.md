# Problem 4.1

## Key concept

For equal force magnitudes, $|\vec F_1+\vec F_2|=\sqrt{2F^2(1+\cos\theta)}$.

## Worked solution

1. Set the resultant magnitude to each stated value.
2. For $2F$, the forces are parallel: $\theta=0^\circ$; for $\sqrt2F$, they are perpendicular: $\theta=90^\circ$; for zero, they are antiparallel: $\theta=180^\circ$.

## Why it works

The angle controls how much of each force reinforces or cancels the other.

## Issue signals

- `force-magnitudes-added-without-direction`
- `vector-cancellation-direction`

# Problem 4.5

## Key concept

Newton's second law is applied componentwise: $\sum\vec F=m\vec a$.

## Worked solution

1. Draw the free-body diagram and resolve the supplied forces into coordinate components.
2. Sum each component and divide by mass.
3. The net-force result is $494\ \text{N}$ at $31.8^\circ$.

## Why it works

Only the vector sum of all external forces determines acceleration.

- `force-components-summed-as-magnitudes`

# Problem 4.9

# Problem 4.11

- `kinematics-factor-of-two-error`

# Problem 4.13

## Key concept

Acceleration is determined by the slope of a velocity-time graph, while force follows from $F=ma$.

## Worked solution

1. Identify the interval with the requested slope.

3. The force is $45.0\ \text{N}$ during $2.0\text{–}4.0\ \text{s}$; the requested zero/interval checks follow directly from the graph.
