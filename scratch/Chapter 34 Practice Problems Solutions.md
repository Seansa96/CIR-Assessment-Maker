# Problem 34.1

## Key concept

A plane mirror forms a virtual upright image the same distance behind the mirror as the object is in front, with unit magnification.

## Worked solution

1. The candle is $39.2\ \text{cm}$ in front of the mirror.
2. Place the virtual image $39.2\ \text{cm}$ behind the mirror.
3. Use $m=+1$ to obtain image height $h_i=4.85\ \text{cm}$.

## Why it works

Plane-mirror reflection makes the reflected rays appear to diverge from a symmetric point behind the mirror without changing object size.

## Issue signals

- `plane-mirror-image-distance-error`
- `plane-mirror-magnification-error`

# Problem 34.3

## Key concept

Each point of an object in front of a plane mirror forms an equally distant virtual point behind the mirror.

## Worked solution

1. The lead tip images $12.0\ \text{cm}$ behind the mirror; the eraser end images $21.0\ \text{cm}$ behind it.
2. The image length is their separation: $21.0-12.0=9.0\ \text{cm}$.
3. The lead-tip image remains closer to the mirror.

## Why it works

The plane mirror preserves distances parallel and perpendicular to its surface through reflection symmetry.

## Issue signals

- `plane-mirror-image-distance-error`
- `image-endpoint-order-reversed`

# Problem 34.5

## Key concept

For a concave mirror, $f=R/2$ and the mirror equation is $1/p+1/q=1/f$; magnification is $m=-q/p$.

## Worked solution

1. Calculate $f=22.0/2=11.0\ \text{cm}$ and use object distance $p=16.5\ \text{cm}$.
2. Solve
$$
\frac1q=\frac1f-\frac1p
$$
for image distance $q$.
3. Calculate $m=-q/p$ and $h_i=mh_o$.
4. A positive $q$ means a real image in front of the mirror; negative magnification means inverted. Draw a parallel ray reflected through $F$ and a center-of-curvature ray retracing its path.

## Why it works

The concave mirror brings reflected rays together for an object outside its focal point, and the ray geometry agrees with the mirror equation.

## Issue signals

- `mirror-focal-length-radius-error`
- `mirror-sign-convention-error`

# Problem 34.7

## Key concept

For an extremely distant object, the image forms essentially at the focal plane and its size is $|h_i|\approx fD/p$.

## Worked solution

1. Convert Mars diameter and Earth–Mars distance to the same length units.
2. Use small-angle approximation $\theta\approx D/p$.
3. At focal length $f=1.75\ \text{m}$, calculate image diameter $|h_i|=f\theta=fD/p$.
4. State that the image is real and inverted at the focal plane.

## Why it works

A distant object subtends a small angle at the telescope mirror, and rays at that angle focus at a proportional height in the focal plane.

## Issue signals

- `small-angle-image-size-ratio-error`
- `astronomical-distance-unit-conversion-error`

# Problem 34.9

## Key concept

For a convex reflecting surface, focal length is negative and the mirror equation plus magnification determines object location and image properties.

## Worked solution

1. The shell’s convex radius gives $f=-R/2=-9.00\ \text{cm}$.
2. The stated image is behind the mirror, so use negative image distance $q=-6.00\ \text{cm}$ under the standard sign convention.
3. Solve $1/p+1/q=1/f$ for the coin’s object distance $p$.
4. Use $m=-q/p$ and $h_i=mh_o$ to determine image size and orientation.
5. Positive magnification gives an upright image; negative $q$ identifies it as virtual.

## Why it works

A convex mirror diverges reflected rays, so their backward extensions meet at an upright, reduced virtual image behind the mirror.

## Issue signals

- `convex-mirror-focal-length-sign-error`
- `mirror-image-distance-sign-error`
