# Problem 6.1

## Key concept

Work is $W=Fd\cos\theta$; forces perpendicular to displacement do no work.

## Worked solution

1. Applied push: $(2.40\ \text{N})(1.50\ \text{m})=3.60\ \text{J}$.
2. Friction opposes motion: $-(0.600)(1.50)=-0.900\ \text{J}$.
3. Normal force and gravity are perpendicular to the horizontal displacement, so each does $0$ work.
4. Net work is $2.70\ \text{J}$.

## Why it works

Only a force component parallel to displacement transfers mechanical energy by work.

## Issue signals

- `work-sign-for-friction`
- `perpendicular-force-does-work`

# Problem 6.3

## Key concept

At constant velocity, net force and net work are zero.

## Worked solution

1. $N=mg$ and $f_k=\mu_kN=(0.25)(30.0)(9.80)=74\ \text{N}$.
2. The worker applies $74\ \text{N}$, so $W_{\rm push}=Fd=333\ \text{J}$.
3. Friction does $-333\ \text{J}$; normal force and gravity do zero work.
4. Net work is zero.

## Why it works

The work-energy theorem requires zero net work when speed is unchanged.

## Issue signals

- `constant-velocity-means-no-applied-force`
- `work-sign-for-friction`
- `normal-force-always-equals-weight`
