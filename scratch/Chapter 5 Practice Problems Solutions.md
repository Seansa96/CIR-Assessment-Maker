# Problem 5.1

## Key concept

An ideal massless rope has uniform tension; the pulley support carries the vector sum of the two rope tensions.

## Worked solution

1. Each hanging $25.0\ \text{N}$ weight is in equilibrium, so rope tension is $25.0\ \text{N}$.
2. The pulley is pulled downward by two rope segments, each of tension $25.0\ \text{N}$.
3. The chain tension is $50.0\ \text{N}$.

## Why it works

The chain supports both downward rope forces, not just one of them.

## Issue signals

- `pulley-support-tension-not-doubled`
- `equilibrium-force-balance`

# Problem 5.3

## Key concept

In a hanging heavy chain, tension at a point supports the weight below that point.

## Worked solution

1. At the bottom, the chain supports only the wrecking ball: $T_{\min}=75.0g=735\ \text{N}$.
2. At the top, it supports ball plus full chain: $T_{\max}=(75.0+26.0)g=990\ \text{N}$.
3. At three-fourths height, include the ball plus three-fourths of the chain weight: $926\ \text{N}$.

## Why it works

Tension varies in a massive rope because different points support different amounts of weight.

## Issue signals

- `massive-rope-tension-constant`
- `supported-mass-fraction-error`
