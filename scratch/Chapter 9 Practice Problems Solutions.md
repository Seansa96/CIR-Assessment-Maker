# Problem 9.1

## Key concept

For circular geometry, $s=r\theta$ when $\theta$ is in radians.

## Worked solution

1. $\theta=s/r=1.50/2.50=0.600\ \text{rad}=34.4^\circ$.

3. $s=r\theta=(1.50)(0.700)=1.05\ \text{m}$.

# Problem 9.51

## Key concept

For geometrically similar objects, mass scales as the cube of the linear scale and moment of inertia as mass times length squared.

## Worked solution

1. Let the scale factor be $f$. Then $m\propto f^3$ and $I\propto mf^2=f^5$.
2. Rotational kinetic energy is $K=\tfrac12I\omega^2$.
3. For corresponding motions at the same angular speed, the full-scale energy is the model energy multiplied by $f^5$.

## Why it works

The two factors of length in $I=\int r^2\,dm$ supplement the three powers of length already contained in the mass.

## Issue signals

- `wrong-geometric-scaling-power`
- `linear-scale-used-for-volume`

# Problem 9.53

## Worked solution

# Problem 9.55

## Key concept

The parallel-axis theorem relates an axis through a corner to the parallel center-of-mass axis.

## Worked solution

1. The central perpendicular axis has $I_{\rm cm}=\tfrac1{12}M(a^2+b^2)$.
2. The center-to-corner distance satisfies $d^2=(a/2)^2+(b/2)^2$.
3. Thus $I_{\rm corner}=I_{\rm cm}+Md^2=\tfrac13M(a^2+b^2)$.

## Why it works

Every part of the sheet is farther from a corner axis than from the parallel central axis.

## Issue signals

- `parallel-axis-term-omitted`
- `center-to-corner-distance-error`

# Problem 9.59

## Key concept

For a continuous object, $I=\int r^2\,dm$.

## Worked solution

1. Divide the disk into concentric rings of radius $r$ and thickness $dr$.
2. With surface density $\sigma=M/(\pi R^2)$, $dm=2\pi r\sigma\,dr$.
3. Integrate $I=\int_0^R r^2(2\pi r\sigma\,dr)=\tfrac12MR^2$.

## Why it works

Each ring has the same distance $r$ from the rotation axis, making $r^2dm$ its exact contribution.

- `integration-power-error`

Superposition works because the integral defining $I$ is linear in mass.

# Problem 9.93

## Key concept

Model the limbs as rods rotating about one end and add their rotational energy to the body's translational energy.

## Worked solution

1. Convert walking speed to m/s and the limb swing angle to radians.
2. Average angular speed is $\omega=\Delta\theta/\Delta t$.
3. Allocate the stated body-mass fractions to the arms and legs, then use $I=\tfrac13mL^2$ for each limb group.
4. Add $\tfrac12I\omega^2$ contributions and compare with $\tfrac12MV^2$.

## Why it works

The limb masses have both whole-body translational motion and motion relative to the body, so both kinetic-energy contributions are real.

# Problem 9.95

## Key concept

For a planar body, the perpendicular-axis theorem is $I_z=I_x+I_y$.

## Worked solution

1. For an element at $(x,y)$, write $dI_z=(x^2+y^2)dm$, $dI_x=y^2dm$, and $dI_y=x^2dm$.
2. Integrate to prove $I_z=I_x+I_y$.
3. Use symmetry to set $I_x=I_y=I_z/2$ for the centered washer and square.
4. Substitute the table values for $I_z$ to obtain the stated in-plane inertias.

## Why it works

Squared perpendicular distance to the $z$ axis is exactly the sum of squared distances to the two in-plane axes.

# Problem 9.97

## Key concept

A nonuniform cylinder requires using the supplied density in both the mass and inertia integrals.

## Worked solution

1. For a cylindrical shell, write $dV=2\pi rL\,dr$ and $dm=\rho(r)dV$.
2. Integrate $M=\int_0^R dm$ to relate the density constant to $M$.
3. Integrate $I=\int_0^R r^2dm$ and eliminate the density constant using the mass result.

## Why it works

The radial density changes how much mass lies at each lever arm, so the uniform-cylinder formula cannot be used directly.

## Issue signals

- `nonuniform-density-treated-as-uniform`
- `cylindrical-volume-element-error`

# Problem 9.99

## Key concept

For a spherically symmetric but nonuniform density, integrate spherical shells about a diameter.

## Worked solution

1. Write the shell mass as $dm=4\pi r^2\rho(r)\,dr$.
2. Use the shell's diameter-axis inertia $dI=\tfrac23r^2dm$.
3. Integrate the supplied density law from $0$ to $R$; separately integrate mass if the result must be expressed in terms of $M$.

## Why it works

Each thin spherical shell has a known central inertia, and summing shells accounts for the changing density exactly.

- `spherical-volume-element-error`
- `nonuniform-density-treated-as-uniform`
